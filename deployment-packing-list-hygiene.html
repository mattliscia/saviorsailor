Hygiene and Toiletries Checklist for Deployment

Everyone has their own preferred brand and scent, so treat this as a check-off list rather than a shopping list. The point is not to forget anything, because the ship's store will not have your brand and the next port might be a month away.

FCA2(SW) Alex Velichko

Founder, Savior Sailor · 8+ years U.S. Navy, 22+ months at sea

Shower and Laundry

30

Shower Shoes

The ship “head” is a public bathroom. To avoid a foot infection you need a barrier between your foot and floor.

This is not optional. Every corpsman on the ship will tell you the same thing, and a pair of slides is the cheapest medical prevention on this list.

View on Amazon
31

Toiletry Bag

Having a carrying case for your toiletries makes life much easier transiting to and from the bathroom. Make sure you can hang it!

There is no counter space in a ship's head. A bag with a hook hangs off the stall or a pipe and keeps your stuff off the deck.

View on Amazon
32

Two Towels

Two towels is preferred because you can use one for the shower and the other for port calls when you hit the beach or pool. Also if it is laundry day or you drop your towel on the “head” (bathroom) floor having a spare is a life saver.

Quick-dry towels are worth the money on a ship, because a cotton towel that never dries turns into a mildew problem in your locker.

View on Amazon
33

Body Wipes

THROW AWAY! DO NOT FLUSH! Sometimes you will be working outside all day and you don’t have time to shower so body wipes will help you stay fresh.

Water hours, secured showers and eighteen-hour days happen. Wipes are the shower you take when there is no shower to take.

View on Amazon
34

Laundry Pods

Pods vs liquid is more compact, portable and easy to carry when transiting to laundry.

A jug of liquid detergent is a spill waiting to happen in a moving locker. Pods in a zip bag take up a fraction of the space and carry to the laundry in one hand.

35

Laundry Backpack

This will make carrying laundry to and from your rack much easier!

Laundry is several ladders and passageways away, and you need both hands on a ship. A drawstring laundry backpack solves that and doubles as storage.

36

Tide To Go Stick

Spilled something on your favorite shirt? Use one of these bad boys.

Laundry day is a week away and your uniform has to pass inspection tomorrow. The stain pen buys you the time.

37

“Battle Roll” (Toilet Paper)

If berthing cleaners forgets to restock the toilet paper or the only available stall doesn’t have any......you can thank me later. Remember to only use single ply so the VCHT (ships plumbing) does not go down.

Every experienced sailor keeps a personal roll in their rack. The single-ply rule is not a joke; a clogged vacuum system takes down heads for the whole compartment.

Air Freshener or Odor Absorber

These are great to put in your rack so things don’t start to smell.

Boots, laundry and a closed curtain add up fast. An odor absorber in the locker and a small freshener in the rack keep your six feet of space livable.
